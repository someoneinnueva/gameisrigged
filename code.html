The Show

How the Show Works

Every episode, Game Is Rigged takes a system that's supposed to work and lays out why it doesn't. Rarely because anyone involved is corrupt — usually because the incentives reward something other than the stated goal.

That covers government programs that end up punishing the people they were built for, business models that profit from friction, and everyday norms that turn people into strategic players whether they mean to or not.

We use economics and game theory to trace the incentives behind the headlines, then explain why the outcome was predictable from the start.

The School Arms Race

No Child Left Behind was supposed to close the achievement gap. Instead, it created a system where teachers erased kids' wrong answers, entire school budgets were gutted for test prep, and the students who needed the most help were deliberately abandoned. We break down Goodhart's Law, Campbell's Law, and how rational people respond to an irrational game.

The Subscription Trap

Free trials that quietly bill you, cancellation flows buried five clicks deep, "are you sure?" screens designed to wear you down — subscription businesses aren't broken, they're working exactly as intended. We break down how companies weaponize inertia and friction against their own customers, and why the incentive to make quitting hard will always beat the incentive to make you happy.

Credit Card Rewards

A card swipe feels like winning, but the reward isn't free. We trace how interchange fees get built into everything you buy, then follow the money to see who actually funds airline miles and cash back. Using basic economics and behavioral psychology, we explain why cards push you to spend more, why lower earners end up subsidizing the rewards of the wealthy, and why saved-up miles can lose half their value overnight.
