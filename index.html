Plea Bargaining

with Clark Neily

In 1791, the Founders dedicated nearly half of the Bill of Rights to a single obsession: making it as hard as humanly possible for the government to lock you in a cage without a public jury trial. Today, that constitutional architecture has essentially vanished. Upwards of 97% of criminal convictions in the United States never see a jury; instead, behind closed doors, defendants waive their constitutional protections and plead guilty in a matter of minutes.

How did the most defendant-friendly trial system in human history turn into what Supreme Court Justice Neil Gorsuch called a "conveyor belt" of mass convictions?

In this episode of The Game Is Rigged, host Anay Mehta unpacks the game theory, expected value math, monopoly pricing, and information asymmetry behind the American plea bargaining machine, and explains why rational, completely innocent people are routinely coerced into confessing to crimes they didn't commit. To understand how this system operates on the ground, Anay sits down with Clark Neily, Senior Vice President for Legal Studies at the Cato Institute and veteran constitutional litigator.
